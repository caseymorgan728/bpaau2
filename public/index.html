Starburst (NetEnt) — 96.1% RTP, Low Volatility

Starburst is the pokie every casino uses in free-spin promos, and for good reason. It is a five-reel, ten-payline space-themed classic with expanding Starburst Wilds that lock and respin. At 96.1% RTP and low volatility, it pays out smaller wins more often, which makes it the ideal pokie for clearing wagering without going bust. If you are turning a no-deposit chip into a withdrawable balance, Starburst is the safest way to grind through the play-through.

Book of Dead (Play'n GO) — 96.2% RTP, High Volatility

Rich Wilde's Egyptian adventure is one of the most copied pokies ever made. Land three Book scatters to trigger ten free spins with a randomly chosen expanding symbol, which is where the big wins live. At 96.2% RTP and high volatility, Book of Dead can go cold for long stretches — but when the free spins land with the right expanding symbol, a single round can pay 5,000x your stake. Mega Moolah (Microgaming) — Progressive Jackpot

Mega Moolah is the pokie that made millionaires. Sweet Bonanza throws out paylines entirely. Wins land anywhere when eight or more matching symbols appear, and the tumbling (Avalanche) mechanic removes winning symbols to let new ones drop in. Pokies Terminology: A Glossary for Australian Players

Pokie terms look like alphabet soup when you are new. Most bonus terms — and most arguments about whether a bonus is actually good — come down to a handful of words. This glossary covers the ten terms you will see on every casino site, in every bonus T&C and in every pokie review. Once you know these, the fine print stops being scary. Pair it with our high-RTP guide and you will be reading terms like a pro.

RTP (Return to Player). The percentage of total wagered money a pokie pays back to players over a very large number of spins. A 96% RTP means that, in theory, $96 of every $100 wagered is returned over time. It is a long-run average — your single session can and will swing far above or below it. Higher RTP generally means better value over hundreds of spins.

Volatility / Variance. How a pokie pays out. Low volatility pays small wins frequently, good for grinding wagering. High volatility pays rarely but bigger, good for chasing a single large hit. Medium sits in between. A high-RTP, high-volatility pokie can still empty your account before the maths catches up.

Paylines. The fixed patterns across the reels where matching symbols must land for a win. Classic pokies have 10–25 paylines; Megaways games replace fixed lines with up to 117,649 ways to win on every spin.

Bonus Rounds / Free Spins. A feature triggered by landing the right symbols — usually scatters — that awards spins you do not pay for, often with extra multipliers, expanding symbols or collector mechanics. Free spins are the heart of most pokie wins.

Progressive Jackpot. A jackpot that grows every time any player anywhere bets on the game, until one lucky spin wins the whole pool. Mega Moolah is the famous example. RTP on progressive slots is usually lower, but the headline prize can be life-changing.

Scatter Symbols. Special symbols that pay out or trigger a feature no matter where they land on the reels — they do not need to be on a payline. Three or more scatters usually unlock free spins or a bonus round.

Wild Symbols. Symbols that substitute for almost any other symbol to complete a winning line, like a joker. Expanding, sticky and walking wilds add extra ways to win. In Starburst, wilds expand to cover a whole reel and respin.

Megaways. A patented reel mechanic by Big Time Gaming that changes the number of symbols on each reel every spin, creating up to 117,649 ways to win. Many popular 2026 pokies — from Bonanza to Fishin' Frenzy — use Megaways.

Hold & Win / Hold and Spin. A bonus feature where certain symbols lock in place for a set number of respins. Every new locked symbol resets the count, and filling the grid usually wins a major or grand jackpot. Wolf Gold uses this mechanic.

Wagering Requirements. The number of times you must bet a bonus (and sometimes the deposit) before winnings can be withdrawn. A $10 bonus with 30x wagering means you must bet $300 total. Always check the wagering — it is the single most important number in any bonus T&C. Our free bonus guide explains how to spot a fair one.
