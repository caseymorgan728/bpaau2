Reels, Paylines & RNG — the 10-Minute Version

An online pokie is a grid of spinning columns called reels. Most modern pokies have five reels and three rows, and symbols land on those reels. A payline is a line running across the reels; land matching symbols on an active payline (usually left to right) and you win. Classic pokies have 10–25 fixed paylines; Megaways titles change the number of ways to win every spin, sometimes up to 117,649. You pick your total stake, hit spin, and the reels settle.

The result of every spin is decided by a Random Number Generator (RNG) — a certified computer algorithm that produces a random outcome, checked independently so the operator cannot tweak it. That means no spin is "due", no machine goes hot or cold, and past results do not predict the next one. The best beginner habit is to treat each spin as independent, set and forget. RTP & Volatility Explained With AUD Examples

RTP (Return to Player) is the long-term percentage of wagered money a pokie pays back. A pokie with a 96% RTP returns $96 for every $100 wagered over millions of spins — the house keeps $4. It is a long-run average, not a promise for your session, but it tells you which titles bleed the slowest. As a beginner, favour pokies with a 96%+ RTP; our best high RTP pokies list shows the loosest in 2026.

Volatility describes the shape of the payouts. Low volatility pays small wins often — great for making a $100 balance last an hour. High volatility pays large wins rarely, with long dry spells that can wipe a small balance in twenty spins. For your first sessions, start low-to-medium volatility. Worked example in AUD: on a $0.50 spin at a medium-volatility, 96.5% RTP pokie, you might win $0.20–$0.80 frequently, occasionally hit a $15–$40 feature, and over 200 spins expect roughly $96.50 back per $100 wagered in the long run — not in one sitting.

Bet Sizing: The 1% Bankroll Rule

The single most valuable money-management habit for a beginner is the 1% rule: never bet more than 1% of your playing bankroll on a single spin. If your session budget is $100, your spin is $1. If it is $200, your spin is $2. This is not about winning more — it is about survival. A low volatility pokie can still drop 20 losing spins in a row; at $1 a spin that costs you $20 and you keep playing. At $10 a spin it costs $200 and the session is over.

Never change the stake mid-losing-run to "win it back" — that is how beginners turn a $100 entertainment budget into a $500 problem. Set the stake once, lock it in, and let the spins play out. If you want to know why this matters alongside bonus play, our bonus terms guide shows how max-bet limits interact with wagering.

One beginner habit pays for itself: keep a simple note of your session budget and your stop number. Decide before you spin what a good session looks like (say, double your deposit, then bank half) and what a bad one looks like (hit your pre-set loss limit, then close the tab). Writing it down removes the emotional "just one more" that ends so many first sessions. Pokies are entertainment — treat the budget like a movie ticket, not an investment.

What does RTP mean on pokies?

RTP (return to player) is the long-term percentage of total stakes a pokie returns to players over millions of spins. A 96% RTP theoretically returns $96 for every $100 wagered, leaving a 4% house edge. It is not a per-session guarantee — you can win or lose big in an hour — but over time higher RTP titles lose your money slower. For 2026 we favour titles around 96–97% for regular play; see our best high-RTP pokies guide for specific numbers.

What is pokie volatility?

Volatility describes the shape of payouts, separate from RTP. Low volatility pays small wins frequently and keeps your balance steady — great for stretching a beginner bankroll. High volatility pays larger wins but rarely, with long dry spells between big hits. Medium volatility sits in between. Beginners should start on low-to-medium volatility pokies to learn the mechanics without brutal swings, then graduate to high-volatile titles once they understand bankroll management. A 96% RTP pokie can be low or high volatility — they are two different dials.

Many new players bet $5–$10 on a $50 balance and bust out within minutes. Stay disciplined, and never chase a loss by increasing your stake.
