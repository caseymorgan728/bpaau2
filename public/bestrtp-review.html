Return to player is the mirror of the house edge: a title published at 96.5% carries a 3.5% edge, and that edge is what the operator expects to keep across millions of spins. It says nothing whatsoever about your next hundred. A 97% game can run stone cold for 200 spins and a 94% one can pay a feature on the third — RTP is the long-run average, not a pacing guarantee. What actually shapes how a session feels is volatility and hit frequency, which are separate figures the studio sets independently. High return with low volatility gives you frequent small wins that tick a wagering requirement along steadily. High return with brutal volatility can empty a bonus balance before the feature ever triggers.

The configurable-RTP point is worth labouring, because it catches experienced players out. The artwork, the paytable and the feature round are identical. Only the maths differs. The only reliable check is the game's own information panel, opened from inside the title after it loads, which is obliged to state the return figure for the build you are playing.
