What Is a Gaming Curacao Licence (Post-2024)?

Gaming Curacao is the official gambling regulator of Curaçao. Historically, four master licence holders (Antillephone, Gaming Services, Curaçao eGaming and the others) sub-licensed operators, which led to a confusing mix of logos and inconsistent enforcement. In 2024 the island consolidated that system under a single, unified Gaming Curacao regulator and registry. If a site today says it holds a "Curaçao" licence, the number should trace back to this one public registry — not to a private master-holder's own website.

A legitimate licence obliges the operator to use certified random number generators (RNGs) for its pokies, segregate player funds from operational money, publish its terms, and submit to periodic audit. It is not the strictest regime in the world — the UK Gambling Commission and Malta's MGA impose heavier capital and complaint rules — but for offshore casinos that accept Australian players, a current, verifiable Gaming Curacao licence is the baseline trust signal. Step-by-Step: Verify a Licence Number in 90 Seconds

Do this before you deposit a single dollar. The process is the same on every reputable site:

1. Find the licence mark in the footer. Scroll to the very bottom of the casino homepage. A genuine site shows the Gaming Curacao logo or text, a licence number (often a code like GLH-OCB-00000 or an equivalent format), and the legal company name behind the brand.
2. Click the seal or copy the number. The licence seal should be a live link to the official Gaming Curacao registry. If it is just a flat image, copy the number and open the registry yourself.
3. Search the official registry. Look up the licence number on the official Gaming Curacao public registry. Do not trust a link the casino shortens or wraps in its own domain.
4. Match three things. The registry record must show (a) the correct licence holder name, (b) the operator's domain listed under that licence, and (c) a status of active, not expired, suspended or revoked.
5. Note the date. Screenshot the record with today's date. If you ever need to raise a dispute, that timestamp is your proof the licence was live when you signed up.

Red Flags: Expired Licences, Hidden Holders & Blacklisted Sub-Licences

Not every "Curaçao" badge is worth the pixels it is drawn on. The first red flag is an expired or suspended registry status — if the number is not active, the operator is gambling illegally, and your money has zero protection. The second is a holder name that does not match the brand: many fly-by-night sites borrow a screenshot of someone else's valid licence. The third is a footer that lists a master-licence name but no actual sub-licence number you can search.

Fourth, watch for blacklisted or historically blacklisted sub-licence holders — groups with a record of voiding winnings, slow-cycling complaints, or changing domains to dodge players. Fifth, a site that pressures you to skip KYC, offers "instant withdrawal with no ID", or hides its terms behind a login is telling you everything.
